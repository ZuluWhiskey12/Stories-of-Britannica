# Professor Ardenfelt Recording / Character Context Packet

## Character Direction

Professor Ardenfelt is severe, brilliant, cruel, and almost never careless. He speaks like a man who considers comfort a poor substitute for preparation. His insults should not sound random. They should sound aimed. Even when he is wrong in method, he believes he is forcing students to survive what softer teachers would let them die from.

The actor should not play him as loud by default. Ardenfelt is most frightening when he is controlled. He can be dismissive, venomous, and arrogant, but beneath that is a professor who sees danger before his students do and hates wasted potential almost as much as he hates weakness.

Later, the story reveals why his reputation matters: Ardenfelt is also the Patrician of Agony. In battle, he does not sound stressed. He sounds offended that an enemy mistook the encounter for a contest. His power should feel institutional, personal, and old. He does not fight like a student. He fights like a lesson plan that learned how to kill.

For recording/reference, the actor should focus on the `ARDENFELT` lines. The `NARRATOR`, `PELA`, `DEAN SAINSTROY`, `GARETH`, `ODELYN`, `LORNE`, `LUCIAN`, and other lines are included as cue/context lines so the scenes do not feel disconnected.

This packet covers Ardenfelt's current scenes through Chapter 9, including his early classroom scenes, Chapter 6 intervention, Mage-Card exam setup, and the roadblock fight from his point of view.

## Scene 8: Panic Wearing Formal Clothing

Context: Ardenfelt explains why the explosions are not an evacuation-level emergency. His point is harsh, but educational.

Tone: Academic contempt. He is irritated that students do not hear the difference between a lesson and an attack.

NARRATOR: The insult lands because there is enough truth in it to hurt.

NARRATOR: He takes her by the arm and drags her toward the east side of the Academy.

ARDENFELT: The Academy has many defense mechanisms, one of which is to detect intruders and alert the staff. Those explosions are from a very simple spell, and the fact that the Explosive Magic Specialization class is being taught at the moment is no accident.

ARDENFELT: If an actual invasion were to happen, the Academy would be alerted. It is a fail-safe that prevents the Nhal or the other royal houses from being able to strike a major blow. As for Explosive Magic Specialization, that is taught every time this year. It's an exam, to see how well the students can control and cast the spell.

PELA: But, why didn't they warn us about it? Why is no one telling the students what is going on?

PELA: If it sounds like an attack, students are going to treat it like one.

ARDENFELT: Only students who listen with fear before knowledge.

ARDENFELT: You are Brittanican mages. You should be able to recognize the sound of an explosion and determine the source. It's not my fault if the students are too lazy to do so.

ARDENFELT: There's a big difference between how combat sounds and a demonstration blast of an explosion. It is a basic thing you should have learned in your first semester... of course, it appears you were not paying attention.

PELA: Well, why not evacuate the building?

ARDENFELT: The building is shielded and reinforced. Evacuating would be pointless, especially considering the Academy has been under attack before and has survived.

ARDENFELT: Enough questions, just observe.
